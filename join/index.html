We are always glad to hear from students and researchers interested in gravitation, quantum field theory in curved spacetimes, and quantum information. What follows should answer most first questions.

Graduate students

Master's and doctoral students join through the graduate program in Physics at UFABC, which admits students through a public selection process (processo seletivo) held periodically. Applications are handled by the program, not by individual advisors, but it is a good idea to write to André Landulfo beforehand to discuss possible topics and confirm availability.

A background in general relativity and quantum mechanics at the level of a physics undergraduate degree is expected. Familiarity with quantum field theory and differential geometry helps but is not a prerequisite — much of it is learned along the way, and the journal club exists partly for that.

Funding

Students in the group are typically supported by scholarships from FAPESP, CAPES, or CNPq. FAPESP fellowships are applied for individually, together with the prospective advisor, and are not tied to the admission process; CAPES and institutional scholarships are usually allocated by the graduate program.

Undergraduates

Undergraduate research projects (iniciação científica) are possible for students at UFABC and can be supported by FAPESP, CNPq, or the university. These are a good way to find out whether this kind of physics suits you before committing to a graduate program.

Visiting and attending the journal club

The InfoGraviton journal club is open. If you would like to attend, or to give a talk, get in touch — see the upcoming seminars for what is coming next.
